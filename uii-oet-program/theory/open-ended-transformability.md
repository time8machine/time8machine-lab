# Principle of Open-Ended Transformability

## Formal statement

Intelligence is the capacity of a physical system to generate, evaluate, and enact transformations of its own organization, representations, and environment while remaining capable of expanding or reorganizing the space of transformations available to it.

Let T_t denote a representation of the transformations accessible to a system at time t. The research target is not simply growth in the number of behaviors. It is evidence that the system can produce or acquire new transformation classes and alter the generative structure from which subsequent transformations arise.

### Closed adaptability

A system selects among transformations substantially specified in advance.

### Open-ended transformability

A system can alter the effective transformation space as a consequence of interaction, feedback, learning, imagination, experimentation, or reorganization.

The hypothesis is not that T becomes literally infinite. The hypothesis is that the system is not completely closed to new transformation classes by its initial specification.

## Necessary distinction

Novel output is not equivalent to novel capability. A random or unprecedented output may remain inside a fixed generative repertoire. OET therefore requires evidence of persistent capability expansion or reorganization.

## Falsifiability

The principle is weakened or rejected if controlled experiments show that apparent transformation-space expansion is fully explained by selection from a fixed repertoire; no reliable operational measure distinguishes OET from ordinary adaptation; or apparent open-endedness disappears under appropriate novelty controls.

UII treats OET as a research hypothesis about intelligence as a physical, informational, developmental process—not as a settled conclusion.
